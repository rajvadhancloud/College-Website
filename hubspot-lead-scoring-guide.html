💡 Section 01

What is Lead Scoring?

Lead scoring is a methodology that assigns a numerical value to each contact in your CRM based on how closely they match your ideal customer profile and how engaged they are with your business. The higher the score, the more likely they are to buy.

The Lead Scoring Formula

Lead Score = Positive Actions & Fit (+points) − Negative Signals & Misfit (−points)
💡

Why Lead Scoring Matters

Without lead scoring, your sales team treats every lead the same — wasting time on cold contacts while hot prospects go cold. Lead scoring ensures reps focus only on the leads most likely to convert, while marketing nurtures everyone else automatically.

🎯

Prioritize Sales Effort

Focus reps on the highest-potential leads every single day.

🤝

Align Sales & Marketing

Define "sales-ready" together with a shared numeric threshold.

📈

Improve Conversion Rates

Timed, relevant outreach to leads at the right moment.

⚙️

Automate Nurturing

Trigger workflows and sequences automatically based on score.

⚖️ Section 02

Types of Lead Scoring in HubSpot

HubSpot offers two scoring models. Choose based on your data maturity and plan level.

🧮

Manual Lead Scoring

  • You define the criteria and assign point values
  • Full control over what matters to your business
  • Works on all HubSpot plans (Starter+)
  • Requires regular manual review and tuning
  • Best when starting out or in niche industries
  • Uses the HubSpot Score property
🤖

Predictive Lead Scoring (AI)

  • AI analyzes patterns across all your contacts
  • Learns from closed/won deals to identify what "good" looks like
  • Requires Professional or Enterprise plan
  • Needs minimum 200 contacts + deal history to train
  • Scores update automatically as new data comes in
  • Uses the Likelihood to Close property
ℹ️

Recommendation

Start with Manual Lead Scoring to establish your baseline criteria and understand what converts in your business. Once you have 6+ months of deal history, layer in Predictive Scoring on top for AI-powered refinement.

📊 Section 03

Scoring Criteria & Points Framework

Lead scoring combines two dimensions: Fit (who they are) and Engagement (what they do). Below is a ready-to-use scoring framework you can adapt for your business.

👤

Demographic Fit

WHO they are — Positive Scores
  • Job Title: Decision Maker (VP, Director, C-Suite)+20
  • Job Title: Manager / Team Lead+10
  • Job Title: Individual Contributor+5
  • Industry matches ICP+15
  • Company size: 50–500 employees+10
  • Company size: 500+ employees+15
  • Located in target geography+10
  • Phone number provided+5
  • Work email (not Gmail/Yahoo)+10
⚡

Behavioral Engagement

WHAT they do — Positive Scores
  • Requested a Demo / Free Trial+30
  • Visited Pricing Page+20
  • Downloaded a Case Study+15
  • Attended a Webinar+15
  • Opened 3+ Marketing Emails+10
  • Clicked Email CTA+8
  • Visited Website 3+ Times+10
  • Downloaded an eBook / Guide+10
  • Submitted a Contact Form+20
📉

Negative Signals

Disqualifiers — Negative Scores
  • Job Title: Student / Intern−15
  • Personal email (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.)−10
  • Unsubscribed from Emails−20
  • Marked Email as Spam−30
  • Industry: Competitor−25
  • No activity in 90+ days−15
  • Bounced Email−10
  • Company size < 10 (if SMB not ICP)−10
  • Visited Careers / Jobs page only−10
⏰

Score Decay Rules

Time-based score reduction
  • No email opens in 30 days−5
  • No website visits in 60 days−10
  • No engagement in 90 days−20
  • Deal Closed Lost−30
  • No activity in 180 days−40
⚠️

Implement Score Decay

Use a recurring HubSpot workflow to subtract points for inactive contacts. Prevents stale leads from clogging your hot pipeline.

✅

Scoring Range Recommendation

Keep your scoring scale between 0 and 100 for simplicity. Design criteria so a perfect ICP who books a demo reaches approximately 80–100 points. This makes threshold definitions intuitive for both marketing and sales teams.

⚙️ Section 04

Step-by-Step Setup in HubSpot

Follow these steps to configure the HubSpot Score property and build your first scoring rules inside HubSpot.

1

Navigate to the Lead Scoring Settings

Find the scoring configuration inside your HubSpot portal settings.

  • Click the Settings ⚙️ icon in the top-right of your HubSpot portal.
  • In the left sidebar, go to Properties.
  • Search for "HubSpot Score" in the property list.
  • Click on HubSpot Score to open the scoring editor.
2

Add Positive Scoring Attributes

Define the criteria that add points to a contact's score when the condition is met.

  • Click "Add criteria" under the Positive attributes section.
  • Select a property (e.g., Job Title, Industry, Form Submission, Page View).
  • Set the condition (e.g., Job Title "contains" Director).
  • Enter the point value for that criterion (e.g., 20 points).
  • Click Save and repeat for each positive criterion.
ℹ️

Available Scoring Criteria Types in HubSpot

  • Contact properties — Job title, industry, country, lifecycle stage, etc.
  • Company properties — Company size, revenue, domain, industry
  • Form submissions — Submitted a specific form (e.g., demo request)
  • Email engagement — Opened, clicked, unsubscribed
  • Page views — Visited a specific URL (e.g., /pricing)
  • List membership — Is/was a member of a specific contact list
  • Workflow enrollment — Was enrolled in a specific automation
3

Add Negative Scoring Attributes

Define criteria that subtract points — these act as disqualifiers.

  • Click "Add criteria" under the Negative attributes section.
  • Add criteria like: Email unsubscribed = true → −20 points.
  • Add: Job Title contains "student" → −15 points.
  • Add: Email bounce reason is known → −10 points.
  • Save each criterion after setting the negative point value.
4

Save and Activate the Scoring Model

Once all criteria are configured, activate the model to start scoring.

  • Review all positive and negative criteria in the editor.
  • Click Save at the top of the scoring editor.
  • HubSpot will immediately begin calculating the HubSpot Score for all existing contacts.
  • Allow 1–4 hours for HubSpot to score all existing records (varies by database size).
5

Verify Scores on Contact Records

Spot-check the scores on known contacts to validate your model is working correctly.

  • Open a contact you know is a strong lead — verify their score is in the high range.
  • Open a contact you know is a poor fit — verify their score is low or negative.
  • On the contact record, find the HubSpot Score property in the left panel.
  • Click the score number to see a breakdown of which criteria are contributing.
6

Enable Predictive Lead Scoring (Pro/Enterprise Only)

Activate AI-powered scoring alongside manual scoring for maximum accuracy.

  • Go to Settings → Properties → Contact Properties.
  • Search for "Likelihood to close".
  • This property is automatically populated by HubSpot AI — no configuration needed.
  • HubSpot requires at least 200 contacts in your CRM with deal associations to begin training.
  • Use this as a secondary score alongside your manual HubSpot Score.

🔁 Section 06

Automation Workflows by Score

The real power of lead scoring is what happens automatically when a contact reaches a threshold. Build these four core workflows in HubSpot to activate your scoring model.

Workflow 1 — MQL Alert & Assignment (Score ≥ 50)

1
Trigger

Contact's HubSpot Score reaches 50+

Enrollment trigger: HubSpot Score ≥ 50 (check every day, re-enroll if score increases again).

2
Action

Update Lifecycle Stage to MQL

Set Contact property: Lifecycle Stage = Marketing Qualified Lead.

3
Notify

Send Internal Notification to Sales Rep

Send in-app + email notification to the contact's assigned owner: "New MQL: [Contact Name] has reached a lead score of [Score]. Review now."

4
Action

Create a Follow-up Task for the Rep

Create a task: "Reach out to MQL within 24 hours" assigned to the contact owner. Due date: tomorrow at 9 AM.

Workflow 2 — SQL Immediate Alert (Score ≥ 75)

1
Trigger

HubSpot Score reaches 75+

High-priority trigger — fires as soon as score crosses 75.

2
Action

Update Lifecycle Stage to SQL

Set Lifecycle Stage = Sales Qualified Lead. If no owner assigned, auto-rotate to next available rep using round-robin rotation.

3
Notify

Urgent Slack / Email Notification

Send urgent notification to rep AND their manager: "🔥 HOT LEAD: [Name] scored [Score]. Call within 4 hours."

4
Action

Enroll in Sales Sequence

Automatically enroll the contact in a Sales Email Sequence (if not already in an active sequence).

Workflow 3 — Score Decay (Inactive Leads)

1
Trigger

Last Activity Date is more than 90 days ago

Scheduled workflow — runs daily. Trigger: Last Activity Date > 90 days ago AND HubSpot Score > 0.

2
Action

Calculate and Reduce Score

Use a Math calculation action to subtract 20 from the current HubSpot Score (minimum 0).

3
Branch

If Score Drops Below 25 — Downgrade Lifecycle

If HubSpot Score is now < 25: Set Lifecycle Stage back to Lead and remove from any active sales sequences.

✅

Workflow 4 — Nurture Cold Leads (Score 0–25)

Build a long-term nurture workflow that enrolls all contacts with score < 25 and sends educational content every 2 weeks. When their score crosses 25 (due to engagement), they are automatically unenrolled from nurture and enrolled in MQL workflows. This creates a fully automated lead progression engine.

🏅 Section 07

Best Practices & Common Mistakes

✅

Do This

  • Start simple — 5–10 criteria, then expand
  • Score both demographic FIT and behavioral ENGAGEMENT
  • Agree on MQL/SQL thresholds with sales before launch
  • Implement score decay for inactive leads
  • Review and tune scores every 30–60 days
  • Use negative scoring to disqualify bad fits
  • Tie scoring thresholds directly to lifecycle stages
  • Build workflows that fire on score milestones
  • Track win rates by score band to validate accuracy
  • Document every criterion and the reason for its point value
❌

Avoid This

  • Too many criteria (50+) that make scores unpredictable
  • Only scoring engagement, ignoring demographic fit
  • Setting thresholds without sales team agreement
  • Never revisiting or tuning the model after launch
  • Ignoring score decay — stale leads inflate the pipeline
  • Using identical point values for all criteria
  • Not tracking which leads actually convert to customers
  • Sending all scored leads directly to sales (skipping MQL)
  • Building complex logic before validating simple rules first
  • No documentation — creates a black box nobody understands
🎯

The 30-Day Review Cycle

After your first 30 days, pull a report of all contacts who scored 75+ and check how many converted to customers. If conversion rate is below 20%, your thresholds are too low. If sales say they're getting too many bad leads, raise the MQL threshold. Iterate monthly until your scoring model reliably predicts closers.

⚠️

Avoid "Score Inflation"

If a contact can trigger the same positive criterion repeatedly (e.g., visit the pricing page 10 times for 10 × 20 = 200 points), your scores become meaningless. HubSpot's scoring tool applies each rule once per contact, but double-check your workflow-based scoring rules to avoid double-counting.
